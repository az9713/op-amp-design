Evidence boundary

This account uses the project records under this folder: 13 early visual-generation records, seven governance surfaces, five specification/audit drafts, 28 durable decision records, 26 weekly graph/manifests/README records, 41 layout overlays, eight review pages, 17 test modules, 78 generated connectivity receipts, the local Roberge PDF and book crops, and the final integrated capstone. Generated SVGs and decks are treated as derived evidence, not independent design authorities.

Coverage: textual and tabular records are substantial and locally addressable; visual evolution is sampled through the historical HTML/SVG surfaces; full conversation transcripts are not present in the folder. File modification times establish an ordering, not authorship.

Attribution note

The early files do not name their model author. Calling the first phase “Grok’s journey” follows the user’s explicit attribution. Everything said about what those files contain is directly observed; attribution of those records to Grok is user-supplied provenance, not a fact encoded in the files themselves.

Part I

The Grok-era journey: increasingly sophisticated drawings without electrical authority

Attempt 1

Hand-authored weekly SVGs

docs/schematics_v1.html and _gen_schematics.py attempted to tell the entire W1–W13 cumulative story with coordinate-authored SVG. The work established the chapter sequence, color language, chassis metaphor, and many recognizably analog symbols. Connectivity, however, lived in geometry: a line touching a symbol looked connected whether or not any electrical model agreed.

Attempt 2

Geometry checking after the drawing

check_schematic_connections.py searched for wires entering op-amp triangles, transistor-body collisions, and unmarked T-junctions. The attempt log records repeated failures and “three-attempt stop” waivers, including Week 8–10 discrete-amplifier drawings. This was useful picture lint, but a waived geometry failure could still allow the checker to exit successfully.

Attempt 3

Textbook crops as visual authority

docs/schematics-other.html and _gen_book_and_schemdraw.py juxtaposed Roberge crops with generated circuits. This was an epistemically valuable move: the book images became a visual specification and exposed where the generated drawings had drifted. But a crop could not express the project’s cumulative physical state, build additions, or a matched SPICE implementation.

Attempt 4

Schemdraw chassis maps

docs/schematics-schemdraw.html and _gen_schemdraw_box.py adopted a schematic library and pin anchors. The diagrams looked more conventional, yet weekly chassis maps still mixed separate modules, empty sockets, inactive hardware, and experiment wiring. Pin anchors improved placement; they did not establish one canonical net.

Attempt 5

One “complete circuit” per sheet

docs/schematics-engine.html, its contact sheet, and _gen_engine.py narrowed each picture to one circuit. This reduced clutter and produced a useful catalog. It still used turtle-style drawing commands: “go right, go up, route around the triangle.” The circuit existed as a sequence of pen moves, not a graph from which SPICE could be generated.

What this phase accomplished

  • Made the cumulative one-chassis concept visible.
  • Established the need for active/inactive/change styling.
  • Collected and cropped relevant Roberge figures.
  • Built a concrete failure corpus rather than hiding bad drawings.
  • Recognized that complex Week 8–10 transistor sheets were the stress test.

Why it could not close

  • No stable component, pin, or net identity.
  • No mechanism forcing SVG and SPICE to describe the same circuit.
  • Junctions and crossings were drawing decorations.
  • Geometric lint could diagnose symptoms but not prove topology.
  • Some conceptual circuits were wrong, most notably the Week 2 sign chain.

The inflection point

“The gap is the representation, not the coordinates.”

docs/schematic-methods.html is the most important Grok-era record because it diagnoses the failure correctly. It contrasts a coordinate program—resistor, line, op-amp—with a net declaration in which vm is one node shared by R1, the inverting input, and Rf. It recommends lcapy/CircuiTikZ first and KiCad second, rejects further Schemdraw patching, and explicitly says one netlist should exist per electrical circuit rather than per chassis picture.

The key limitation: this document reached the correct architectural principle but did not implement it. It still proposed a renderer as the likely source of truth. The later system instead made a project-owned circuit graph the authority and treated every renderer—including lcapy or KiCad—as a replaceable projection.

Part II

The Codex journey: freeze intent, prove architecture, then scale chronologically

Pre-flight

Turn preferences into binding invariants

docs/preflight-decisions.html recorded sixteen decisions: complete end-of-week state, strict cumulative inheritance with honest removals, one large sheet unless clutter demands detail, hidden op-amp power pins under an infrastructure contract, symbolic values plus a build table, ±15 V historical operation, LM301A baseline, and SVG/SPICE identity. It also made four deferred projects durable: patch cords, low-voltage redesign, modern parallel implementations, and construction documentation.

Plan

Do not scale before the hard proof

docs/implementation-plan.html defined gated phases and selected Week 9 as the vertical proof because it combines transistor hierarchy, compensation variants, cumulative state, main/detail sheets, and simulation. docs/project-controls.html separated verified, derived, proposed, blocked, generated, and deferred claims.

Specification

Read the PDF before encoding circuits

Three weekly specification annexes, a cross-range review, and a requirements audit forced contradictions into the open. The result was the weekly electrical specification and Gate 0 identity register, approved through G0-01 to G0-15.

Architecture

Choose a canonical graph, not a drawing package

ADR-001 selected schema-validated JSON, deterministic semantic SVG and SPICE projectors, separate layout overlays, and independent round-trip parsers. A small inverter probe generated byte-identical results twice. Gate 1 approval authorized the Week 9 proof and installation of pinned ngspice 47.

Vertical proof

Transcribe Figure 9.1 twice and let failure remain failure

Transcription A/source map and Transcription B were independently compared in the diff. The pipeline passed schema, graph↔SVG↔SPICE connectivity, and human visual review. It executed eight ngspice cases—but the balance, inverter-gain, and integrator-polarity assertions failed. Gate 2 therefore remained blocked rather than being cosmetically promoted.

Style acceptance

Separate presentation approval from electrical approval

The user accepted the Week 9 visual language: black active circuitry, coral changes, grey installed-inactive hardware, explicit junctions and insulated crossings, large main sheets, and pin-level details when needed. That acceptance allowed chronological topology production without erasing the failed electrical gate.

Production

Rebuild W00–W13 as cumulative graph states

Production proceeded in review batches: W00–04, W05–06, W07–08, W09 reconciliation, then W10, W11, W12, and W13. Every approval froze topology and presentation while carrying forward electrical limitations.

Integration

Publish only after all weekly reviews

After Week 13 acceptance, 44 approved sheets with matched SPICE and connectivity links were embedded in capstone.html. The final integration check found every referenced artifact present, every embedded receipt passing, JavaScript syntax valid, and 113 tests passing.

The electrical contract

How the circuit schematics are grounded

“Grounded” here does not mean “looks like the textbook.” It means each visible electrical claim can be traced through an evidence and representation chain, and each layer has a deliberately limited authority.

1 · Sources and decisions

Roberge figures/equations, primary device data, user approvals, and explicit proposed/derived additions.

2 · Canonical graph

Stable component IDs, logical pins, named nets, values, hierarchy, physical state, weekly inheritance, and variants.

3 · Controlled projections

Semantic SVG plus SPICE emitted from the same resolved graph. Layout overlays may position—but never connect—parts.

4 · Independent equivalence

Parse actual SVG terminal metadata and actual SPICE terminal order; compare both back to the graph.

5 · Separate gates

Schema, connectivity, visual publication, and electrical performance are scored independently.

1. Source grounding

The PDF is read figure-by-figure and section-by-section. Where a book figure is conceptual rather than build-ready, the project labels added parts and values as proposed or derived. A value is not upgraded to “Roberge specified this” merely because it makes the circuit work. Week 9 was transcribed twice; disagreements were adjudicated before encoding.

2. Identity and cumulative-state grounding

A physical object keeps one identity across weeks. A socket, its occupant, and its passive network are distinct. Week N inherits Week N−1 and applies a replayable delta. Installed-but-inactive, removed-off-circuit, reserved-unpopulated, and configuration-only fixtures are different states. This is why Week 7 can truthfully show a nonfunctional channel and Week 10 can retain unused passive-load parts without leaving them electrically parallel to the mirror.

3. Projection grounding

The JSON schema and validator reject unknown pins/nets, duplicate identities, illegal inheritance, hierarchy errors, and invalid state transitions. The SVG renderer writes semantic terminal and net identifiers into the file. The SPICE projector owns the terminal order for each primitive and hierarchical subcircuit. A separate layout JSON can move symbols and specify routing corridors, but it cannot invent a component, wire, value, pin, or model.

4. Equivalence grounding

The connectivity receipt is not a comment written by the generator saying “trust me.” The verifier parses the emitted SVG and emitted SPICE, reconstructs component/pin/net maps, and compares them to the resolved canonical graph. Main and detail sheets share the same module-port boundary. Missing terminals, wrong subcircuit pin counts, SVG-only wires, and SPICE-only elements fail tests.

5. Simulation and claim grounding

ngspice execution is downstream of connectivity proof, not a substitute for it. Ideal/topology models can verify deck construction and qualitative topology but cannot validate historical drift, matching, bandwidth, thermal behavior, or compensation margins. Realistic models require lawful provenance, version/hash records, package mappings, and characterization. The Week 9 proof’s three failed electrical assertions remained visible through every later acceptance record.

6. Human visual grounding

Machine-equivalent connectivity can still render badly. Human review checks overlap, clipping, ambiguous crossings, label collisions, hierarchy readability, desktop/print presentation, and whether the sheet faithfully communicates the week’s actual state. Visual acceptance is therefore necessary—but it is not electrical proof.

A current example of the boundary

The Week 0 dummy-load resistor is connected correctly and drawn badly

The current Week 0 infrastructure SVG draws a black PGND route over the red 3.3 kΩ dummy-load symbol. That is a publication-layout defect caused by placing the PGND trunk inside the resistor’s symbol span.

The electrical evidence nevertheless agrees: the canonical graph assigns the right-hand INF.RDUMMY_N upper pin to PGND and lower pin to N15; the SVG terminal metadata says the same; and the SPICE deck connects the 3.3 kΩ resistor between PGND and N15. The receipt passes.

Conclusion: topology is confirmed; presentation is not acceptable at that location. This is precisely why connectivity and visual gates must remain separate.

Bottom line

The Grok-era work was not wasted: it made the project visible, accumulated a failure corpus, and articulated the right diagnosis. The decisive change was to stop treating a schematic as artwork whose connectivity could be inspected afterward. The Codex phase made the circuit graph primary, made SVG and SPICE disposable but checkable views, and kept source uncertainty, topology acceptance, visual acceptance, and electrical performance as separate claims.

The result is much harder to fool: an electrically correct graph can still expose a bad drawing—as the Week 0 overlap now demonstrates—and a simulator can execute successfully while the electrical acceptance gate still fails—as Week 9 demonstrates. That separation is the project’s central engineering achievement.
