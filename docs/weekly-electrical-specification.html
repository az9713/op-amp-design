01 · Contract

What every weekly release must mean

  • The physical project state is cumulative: Week N inherits Week N−1 and applies an explicit delta.
  • The active configuration is electrically complete and explicit. Physical cable routing is deferred; electrical nets are not.
  • Installed but inactive hardware remains present and is visually subdued.
  • The main weekly view is one large publication-quality electrical sheet when legible; dense pin-level regions move to linked detail sheets with matching boundary pins.
  • Rendering semantics are fixed: retained active circuitry black, new weekly additions coral, and retained inactive circuitry grey. Color never changes electrical meaning.
  • Removal, replacement, temporary fixtures, and nonfunctional states are shown honestly.
  • LM301A is the primary historical stock op-amp. A 741-class device is a declared alternative, never a silent substitute.
  • Symbolic schematics receive recommended-value tables whose claims are labeled verified, derived, proposed, or TBD.
  • SVG and SPICE are later projections of the same canonical graph and must match component/pin/net connectivity.
Integration rule

The detailed range drafts are evidence-bearing annexes. This page is the cross-week decision surface. When this summary and an annex conflict, the issue remains open until resolved; the summary does not silently override source evidence.

The authoritative cross-range identity dictionary, physical manifest, configuration registry, and transition rules are in gate-0-integration-register.html. Provisional IDs in the draft annexes are not canonical aliases.

02 · Foundation

Week 0 through Week 4

W00

Permanent ±15 V infrastructure

External isolated supply interface, split grounds, bypassing, protection placeholders, and test points.

Review

Learning objective: establish a safe, measurable dual-rail and grounding foundation whose limits carry into every later circuit.

Active configuration and delta

Commission unloaded rails and a symmetric dummy load. Establish hidden-power-pin endpoints. No signal-processing amplifier is installed.

Source and verification

Roberge Fig. 11.7 and §§11.3.1–11.3.3; TI LM301A ±15 V conditions. Verify polarity, single ground tie, local droop, startup, and dummy-load current.

W01

Computing core installed

INV1, SUM1, and INT1 retained; INT2 and INT3 positions reserved.

Review

Learning objective: build and independently verify inversion, weighted summation, and integration.

Configurations

  • Inverter test
  • Weighted-summer test
  • Integrator test

Source and candidate baseline

Figs. 1.2a and 1.4, §1.2.3; 30 pF LM301A compensation. Proposed unity resistor pairs and 10 ms integrator remain Gate 0 candidates.

W02

First-order closed loop

No new installed hardware; Week 1 blocks receive an explicit stable-loop configuration.

Blocked: sign

Learning objective: realize a correctly signed first-order differential equation and relate its time constant to waveform decay.

Provisional configuration

INT1.OUT → SUM1 → INV1 → INT1.IN gives the correct sign for dx/dt = −x/τ. A SUM+INT two-block loop has the wrong sign.

Source limitation

Figs. 2.2, 2.3, and 2.9 explain feedback and modeling but are not construction schematics. Open-loop and loading tests require explicit safe fixtures.

W03

Transient and compensation characterization

Measure mutually exclusive 12 pF, 30 pF, and 220 pF LM301A compensation states.

Blocked: fidelity

Learning objective: observe how compensation changes stability and transient response while distinguishing the reference fixture from the cumulative loop.

Configuration conflict

An unchanged Week 2 loop can study compensation but cannot be called an exact repeat of Fig. 3.1, which is a 4.7 kΩ unity inverter driven by a −50 mV step.

End state

One selected compensation capacitor remains installed under a declared stability/settling criterion; alternatives are not drawn in parallel.

W04

Second integrator and second-order loop

Populate INT2; retain INV1 and the empty INT3 position.

Blocked: equation

Learning objective: realize a specified second-order equation and distinguish natural frequency, damping, and marginal stability.

Provisional configuration

A SUM1–INT1–INT2 loop forms a harmonic oscillator with ω0=√(K/(τ1τ2)). In the ideal model it is already on the imaginary axis for every positive K.

Missing definition

The capstone supplies no damping term, concrete ODE, sign convention, or loop-injection fixture, so “find the gain that puts it on jω” is not yet well-defined.

03 · Plant and discrete amplifier

Week 5 through Week 9

W05

Physical regulator plant

Computer retained; symbolic Figure 5.3 regulator installed as a separately active system.

Blocked: build design

Learning objective: connect loop shaping to a safe regulator whose load, dominant pole, and operating limits are measurable.

Configurations

  • Regulator load/capacitance sweep
  • Conditional computer-loop lead
  • Conditional computer-loop lag

Missing engineering

Output/current targets, pass device, unregulated source, SOA, thermal protection, load-discharge path, disturbance injector, and safe loop injection are not specified by Fig. 5.3.

W06

Persistent Schmitt–integrator oscillator

Computer and regulator retained; oscillator becomes a persistent test-source module.

Blocked: persistence

Learning objective: derive and verify relaxation-oscillator amplitude and frequency from thresholds, limiting, and integration parameters.

Recommended delta

Populate the reserved INT3 channel as the oscillator integrator and add a Schmitt stage. Reusing INT1 or INT2 conflicts with later persistence and comparison requirements.

Source and values

Figs. 6.14, 12.7, and 12.8. Limiter choice, target amplitude, exposed modulation port, and reconciliation of the examples remain open.

W07

Intentionally incomplete discrete input stage

Remove the INT1 LM301A; retain the socket; install only the Figure 9.1-compatible input pair and temporary balance network.

Blocked: experiment

Learning objective: characterize differential-pair offset, balance, and drift without attributing full-amplifier behavior to an incomplete channel.

Honest state

The pair has no complete amplifier output and cannot close the integrator feedback-capacitor loop. INT1 is explicitly nonfunctional.

Valid measurements

Pair offset, balance, drift, and collector behavior may be measured with a declared high-impedance fixture. The claimed hold-capacitor wander cannot be attributed to the unfinished amplifier.

W08

Transitional discrete channel

Extend the Week 7 pair with high-gain, load, and output stages.

Blocked: topology

Learning objective: bring up successive amplifier stages while tracking bias, gain, loading, and temporary versus final connections.

Source limitation

Figs. 8.8, 8.13, and 8.27 are separate instructional circuits. Their collage is not automatically Figure 9.1.

Recommended rule

Define Week 8 as an exact component/pin/net subset of the accepted Figure 9.1 graph, with temporary parts explicitly identified and a safe provisional compensation strategy.

W09

Figure 9.1 amplifier and vertical proof

Complete the exact illustrative amplifier and restore it as INT1 after measurement configurations.

Blocked: transcription

Learning objective: validate a complete discrete operational amplifier from transistor graph through compensated closed-loop integrator behavior.

Configurations

  • Compensation-response tests
  • Figure 9.8 inverter/slew fixture
  • Restored cumulative integrator

Gate

Double-transcribe the full Figure 9.1 component/pin/net graph, select lawful device models, define fixtures and parasitics, and select a provisional end-state Cc before the Week 9 proof.

04 · Applications and compensation

Week 10 through Week 13

W10

Current-repeater improvement

Modify the accepted Week 9 discrete amplifier without changing unrelated chassis modules.

Blocked: mapping

Learning objective: quantify how a current-repeater change alters gain, headroom, bias, and large-signal behavior.

Primary delta

Map Figure 10.9’s PNP current repeater into the exact Week 9 first-stage-load interface and derive its reference current/headroom.

Optional branch

Figure 10.25 FET followers should remain a separate optional INT2 configuration unless explicitly promoted into the cumulative baseline.

W11

Three-mode integrators and amplifier measurements

Add reset/operate/hold hardware, characterize the discrete amplifier, and retain the precision rectifier.

Blocked: interface

Learning objective: operate explicit reset/compute/hold states and measure offset and bias currents with topology-faithful fixtures.

Modes and measurements

Use Figs. 12.17/12.18 for switch states and Fig. 11.2 for offset/bias tests. Mode nets and control states must be explicit.

Rectifier correction

Source reinspection established that Figure 11.18 is the grounded half-wave precision rectifier. The floating-load full-wave bridge is Figure 11.19; the older local image named for 11.18 is mislabeled and is not electrical authority.

W12

Three separate complete problem configurations

One cumulative chassis; Butterworth, Van der Pol, and regulator-twin configurations remain electrically distinct.

Blocked: multiplier/twin

Learning objective: reconfigure one machine into linear filtering, nonlinear dynamics, and plant-model experiments without conflating their graphs.

Configuration set

  • Butterworth: SUM becomes integrating summer; populate/reuse INT3; retain INV.
  • Van der Pol: two integrators plus a buildable four-quadrant multiplier path.
  • Regulator twin: derive signs/scales from the actual built plant.

Corrections

Figure 11.28 alone is two-quadrant and unsuitable for bipolar Van der Pol states. The capstone’s “11.28 or 12.9” options are not electrically interchangeable.

W13

Two separate compensation campaigns

Final cumulative state; never overlay mutually exclusive compensation networks.

Derived after measurement

Learning objective: synthesize and verify compensation from measured loop dynamics on explicitly named DUTs.

Campaigns and graph split

  • Fixed dynamics: separate SUM1 lag, INV1 lag, regulator load-capacitance baseline, and compensated regulator graphs.
  • Changed dynamics: separate AMP1 and LM301A INT2 one-pole tests, plus a REG1 error-amplifier two-pole graph.

Constraint

No Figure 13.21 values may be copied into the regulator. Values must be derived from the built plant, measured loop, tolerances, and target crossover. Load-capacitance work is nonapplicable unless G0-15 accepts that DUT case.

05 · Gate 0 decisions

Choices required before circuit encoding

G0-01
Week 0 supply boundaryChoose external supply class, connector, protection philosophy, and grounding/earth assumption.
RecommendationExternal isolated regulated current-limited ±15 V bench supply; keyed low-voltage connector; no internal mains wiring.
G0-02
Week 2 correct signThe stated two-block SUM+INT loop cannot realize stable dx/dt=−x when both blocks invert.
RecommendationApprove the three-block SUM→INV→INT loop and revise the wording/source claims.
G0-03
Week 3 experiment fidelityExact Fig. 3.1 and “leave the loop unchanged” are incompatible.
RecommendationUse a separately labeled Fig. 3.1 reference configuration, then restore and characterize the cumulative loop.
G0-04
Week 4 mathematical objectiveA pure ideal two-integrator oscillator is already on jω; varying positive gain changes frequency, not damping.
RecommendationSpecify a damped second-order equation with an adjustable damping coefficient, then define the marginal-stability experiment.
G0-05
Week 5 regulator targetA build-ready pass stage cannot be selected without output voltage/current and source limits.
RecommendationApprove a low-power teaching target—provisionally 10 V nominal and tens of milliamps—with final values derived under SOA/thermal review.
G0-06
Week 6 oscillator persistenceReusing INT1 or INT2 conflicts with later cumulative use.
RecommendationPopulate the reserved INT3 channel as the dedicated oscillator integrator; allow active repurposing in separate Week 12 configurations.
G0-07
Week 7 invalid hold claimAn input pair alone cannot drive the hold capacitor as an integrator output.
RecommendationDeclare INT1 nonfunctional; measure pair offset/drift with a high-impedance temporary fixture; remove the hold-wander acceptance claim.
G0-08
Weeks 7–9 topology continuityChapter 8 concept figures do not automatically compose into Figure 9.1.
RecommendationMake Weeks 7 and 8 explicit incremental subsets of a double-transcribed exact Figure 9.1 graph.
G0-09
Week 10 FET optionFigure 10.25 would create a second branch on INT2 and complicate the cumulative baseline.
RecommendationKeep it as an optional separate configuration; do not add it to the primary baseline.
G0-10
Week 11 rectifier roleFigure 11.18 has a floating load and may not directly serve a grounded Wien-control interface.
RecommendationBuild/specify the exact bridge as a retained module; defer any ground-referenced control interface until the Wien project is activated.
G0-11
Week 12 four-quadrant multiplicationVan der Pol states are bipolar; Figure 11.28 alone is insufficient.
RecommendationEvaluate a historical four-quadrant method and a modern ±15 V multiplier as explicit alternatives; choose one after complexity/model review.
G0-12
Week 12 integrator useButterworth needs four integrators plus an inverter and changes SUM into an integrating summer.
RecommendationUse SUM+INT1+INT2+INT3 as four integrators and retain INV; treat this as a separate active configuration rather than permanent simultaneous wiring.
G0-13
Week 12 SUM/INV/INT3 passive reconfigurationThe fixed Week 1 networks and Week 6 oscillator wiring cannot acquire Butterworth functions through wires alone.
RecommendationAdd selectable integrating/coefficient branches to SUM1, a Butterworth coefficient branch to INV1, and a distinct INT3 Butterworth network; open incompatible ordinary and oscillator branches in that configuration.
G0-14
Week 12 regulator-twin ownershipAbstract twin blocks would hide extra amplifiers and corrupt the physical/load manifest.
RecommendationUse retained SUM1 and INT2 as configuration roles, with OSC1 as source and REG1 active beside them; add only derived coefficient passives unless the plant equation proves more hardware is required.
G0-15
Week 13 exact DUT and loop ownershipCampaign names alone can hide different electrical graphs and make load compensation appear mandatory.
RecommendationUse separate SUM1 and INV1 fixed-lag graphs; separate AMP1 and DEV.U_INT2 one-pole graphs; use REG1.U_ERR for the regulator two-pole graph; create regulator CLOAD baseline/compensated graphs only if the accepted REG1 loop requires that experiment.

06 · Delegated TBDs

Engineering work that need not block conceptual approval

If Gate 0 approves the recommended direction, the following remain controlled design tasks rather than unresolved project goals:

  • Exact resistor/capacitor values, tolerances, voltage ratings, device types, thermal design, and protection sizing.
  • Safe loop-gain injection and open-loop measurement fixtures.
  • LM301A macro-model provenance, historical transistor-model availability, and named generic substitutes where necessary.
  • Week 5 regulator pass-stage design and regulator-twin coefficient derivation.
  • Week 8/9 pin-by-pin topology transcription and provisional compensation selection.
  • Week 10 current-repeater operating point and insertion map.
  • Week 11 switch types, drive circuits, and available modern equivalents.
  • Week 13 compensation values derived only after the accepted plant/loop models exist.

Each task remains subject to the three gates: connectivity, electrical behavior, and visual fidelity.

07 · Deferred ledger

Preserved future work

  • D-01 — trigger: begin physical patch-cord paths, labels, and routing drawings after canonical weekly nets, connection points, and released cumulative schematics stabilize. Electrical nets remain explicit now.
  • D-02 — trigger: begin the separate low-voltage/rail-to-rail redesign after the ±15 V historical reference passes its three gates and the new operating goals are written.
  • D-03 — trigger: create full parallel modern schematics after the historical implementation is verified and a substitute is shown to materially change topology, range, compensation, or teaching outcome.
  • D-04 — trigger: begin board placement, harness routing, panel mechanics, grounding geometry, and construction photography after circuit, connector, grounding, thermal-loss, and parts definitions stabilize. Electrical safety, ratings, heat dissipation, and grounding constraints remain in scope now; only their physical realization is deferred.

08 · Gate boundary

What approval would authorize

Gate 0 was approved on 2026-08-15 and authorizes Phase 2 toolchain evaluation and canonical-model prototyping. It does not approve unreviewed recommended values, waive safety analysis, or permit a schematic/netlist mismatch.

  • Accept the detailed annexes as source evidence subject to cross-range ID normalization.
  • Authorize engineering TBDs to proceed under documented tests rather than silent inference.
  • Authorize Phase 2 only after the issue ledger records the final choices.
