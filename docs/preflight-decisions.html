01 · Purpose

One physical project, legible at every weekly state

The capstone describes a single analog-computing chassis assembled progressively over multiple weeks. The required visual deliverable is one or more publication-quality electrical schematics per week that faithfully depict the project at the end of that week.

North-star outcome

A learner must be able to read a week’s schematic, understand what physically exists, identify what changed, reproduce the active experiment, and trust that the corresponding SPICE netlist describes exactly the same electrical circuit.

This is a build-ready teaching project. It is neither a collection of disconnected textbook examples nor a conceptual block-diagram course. Pedagogical clarity and construction fidelity are co-equal requirements.

02 · Boundary

Decision record only

Do not implement yet

This document does not authorize circuit redesign, netlist authoring, SVG generation, SPICE simulation, modification of capstone.html, dependency installation, or replacement of the existing schematic pipeline.

The next permissible artifact is a week-by-week schematic specification. Actual circuit and rendering implementation begins only after that specification is reviewed and approved.

03 · Progression

Cumulative physical state, selectively emphasized

The physical chassis evolves as a strict superset of the previous week wherever the project permits. Presentation may suppress visual clutter, but it must not falsify the physical state.

STATE 01

Physically retained

Previously installed circuitry remains represented. Inactive subsystems may be visually subdued, not silently erased.

STATE 02

Actively configured

The circuit used for the week’s experiment receives normal visual weight and remains electrically complete.

STATE 03

New or changed

Additions, rewiring, replacements, and changed values are highlighted in coral.

STATE 04

Removed or incomplete

Physical removals and temporarily nonfunctional channels are shown honestly with explicit state annotations.

Superset rule and exceptions

  • Each week inherits the prior project state and applies an explicit delta.
  • A component that remains on the chassis remains part of the project state even when it is not active in that week’s experiment.
  • Installed but inactive circuitry is rendered in a lighter grey when showing it normally would obscure the active circuit.
  • Physical removal is modeled as a state transition, not hidden to preserve a fictional mathematical superset.
  • For example, when the INT1 IC is pulled, the socket remains and receives an annotation such as “LM301A removed”; the replacement circuitry is shown in its true end-of-week condition.
  • A temporarily incomplete or nonfunctional channel must look incomplete or nonfunctional. The drawing must not anticipate a later week’s finished circuit.
Presentation exception

One large electrical sheet is preferred. If cumulative complexity harms legibility, the main sheet may use a named module boundary accompanied by a pin-for-pin detail sheet. The main sheet must still preserve the complete electrical context.

04 · Source of truth

The SVG and SPICE netlist must match

Non-negotiable identity rule

The schematic and SPICE netlist are two representations of one canonical circuit graph. They may not be independently authored and reconciled by visual inspection.

01

Canonical graph

Components, stable identifiers, pins, named nets, parameters, and weekly state.

02

Schematic view

Publication layout and state styling derived from canonical connectivity.

03

SPICE view

Ideal and realistic simulation variants derived from the same connectivity.

04

Equivalence check

Automated comparison of components, pins, and nets before publication.

Required invariants

  • Every electrical component has one stable identity across the canonical model, schematic, values table, and netlist.
  • Every connected schematic pin maps to exactly one canonical net.
  • No SVG-only wire, SPICE-only component, decorative junction, or implicit electrical connection is permitted.
  • Intentional simulation substitutions—such as an ideal op-amp replacing an LM301A macro-model—must be explicit model variants, not topology drift.
  • Weekly inheritance and deltas must be machine-auditable.
  • The build must fail when schematic and netlist connectivity differ.

05 · Visual contract

Conventional analog notation, optimized for teaching

  • Use conventional publication-quality analog schematic notation.
  • Prefer left-to-right signal flow, feedback above the forward path, vertical supply structure, named nets, and explicit junction dots.
  • Use black for carried-forward active circuitry, coral for the current week’s additions or changes, and light grey for physically retained but inactive circuitry.
  • Prefer one large electrical sheet per week. Add detail sheets only when needed for legibility.
  • Detailed sheets must expand named module boundaries pin-for-pin; they are not alternative or simplified circuits.
  • Hidden op-amp supply pins are allowed on weekly sheets when the permanent infrastructure sheet defines their connections.
  • Use symbolic designators and parameters on schematics, supported by a recommended-values table.
  • Embed final SVG schematics into capstone.html only after the specification and proof pipeline are approved.

Week 0 decision

Week 0 will receive a permanent infrastructure sheet covering the dual ±15 V supply interface, ground architecture, local decoupling, protection, and connector conventions. Weekly experiment sheets may reference this infrastructure rather than repeat it.

06 · Engineering standard

Faithful to Roberge, completed for construction

Roberge’s topology is the electrical and pedagogical reference. Where the textbook omits construction details, the primary schematic will add the necessary practical components directly.

  • Practical additions must be visually identified and documented as implementation additions.
  • Quantities may remain symbolic where instructional variation matters, but a recommended-values table must make the circuit buildable.
  • Primary operation remains at ±15 V.
  • Historical parts and behavior are preserved as the principal learning path.
  • Notes may identify modern, obtainable substitutes that preserve ±15 V behavior.
  • A second full drawing is required only when a modern implementation differs substantially from the historical topology.
  • Test points, expected measurements, loading assumptions, stability-sensitive parts, and component-matching requirements belong in the weekly specification.
Ideal and realistic simulation

Every suitable weekly circuit will have an ideal SPICE variant for topology verification and a realistic variant for bandwidth, compensation, slew rate, saturation, bias current, loading, and other device-level behavior.

07 · Acceptance

Three gates, all required

GATE 01

Connectivity

Mechanically verify every component, pin, net, junction, weekly inheritance rule, and SVG–SPICE equivalence.

GATE 02

Electrical behavior

Use simulation and analytical checks to verify the intended operating point, transfer behavior, stability, and experiment.

GATE 03

Visual fidelity

Compare with the cited Roberge figures and inspect manually for correct notation, readable layout, and honest weekly state.

RELEASE

Publish only after all three

Passing geometry or rendering checks alone is not evidence of electrical correctness.

09 · Deferred ledger

Recorded so these projects do not disappear

D-01

Patch-cord configuration drawings

Defer the physical front-panel cable paths, cable labels, and routing diagrams. Preserve them as a named future companion layer. This deferral does not hide electrical connectivity: canonical nets and schematic wires remain explicit in the first implementation.

D-02

Low-voltage redesign

Create a separate future project for low-voltage or rail-to-rail operation. It must not silently alter the primary ±15 V curriculum. This item is explicitly durable and must appear in subsequent project specifications and handoffs.

D-03

Full modern alternative schematics

The first project records modern component substitutions and preserves ±15 V behavior. Complete parallel modern drawings are deferred unless a substitution materially changes topology or operation.

D-04

Physical chassis and wiring documentation

Board placement, harness routing, front-panel layout, grounding geometry, thermal layout, and construction photographs are outside the first electrical-schematic pass. The Week 0 infrastructure schematic remains in scope.

11 · Planned sequence

Specification before rendering

  1. Write the week-by-week schematic specification. Inventory the end state, inherited hardware, active experiment, circuit boundary, sources, components, nets, state changes, symbolic parameters, recommended values, historical notes, modern notes, and verification targets.
  2. Review contradictions and missing engineering information. Resolve ambiguities between the capstone narrative, Roberge figures, cumulative physical state, and build-ready requirements.
  3. Select the canonical circuit representation. Evaluate candidate tools against the circuit–netlist identity rule; do not choose on drawing aesthetics alone.
  4. Prove the pipeline on a difficult week. Week 9 is the recommended proof because it exercises the evolving discrete amplifier, compensation, cumulative state, detail sheets, ideal simulation, and realistic simulation.
  5. Run all three gates. Verify connectivity identity, electrical behavior, and visual fidelity before scaling.
  6. Generate the remaining weeks. Preserve explicit inheritance and weekly deltas; do not independently redraw each week.
  7. Integrate approved SVGs into the capstone. Modify capstone.html only after the weekly outputs pass their gates.

12 · Authorization

Ready-to-implement checklist

Implementation should not begin until the following preconditions are satisfied:

  • The week-by-week schematic specification has been written and approved.
  • Every week has an explicit end state, active experiment, inherited state, and delta.
  • Every circuit cites its Roberge figure or section and records practical additions.
  • Recommended values and unresolved engineering questions are visible.
  • The canonical representation can generate or mechanically validate both SVG connectivity and SPICE connectivity.
  • The Week 9 proof plan defines ideal and realistic acceptance tests.
  • Deferred items D-01 through D-04 are carried into the next specification or handoff.
  • The user explicitly authorizes implementation after reviewing the specification.
