Build the computer. Then put your amp inside it.

Same box every week. A new module or a socket swap. Nothing is a one-off lab. The finished machine solves an ODE, runs a model of a real regulator, and uses an operational amplifier you designed. The approved weekly SVG schematics and their matched SPICE decks now live directly beside each build step below.

The source is James K. Roberge’s MIT textbook, Operational Amplifiers: Theory and Practice. Read the MIT OpenCourseWare PDF or the LibreTexts edition. Added build values and fixtures are identified as project-derived or proposed rather than silently attributed to the book.

Finished machine

Analog computer + plant + discrete op-amp

Patchbay of summers, inverters, and three-mode integrators. A Figure 5.3 regulator lives on the same chassis as a physical plant. A Schmitt–integrator oscillator is the test source. Mid-build, one integrator IC is pulled and replaced by the two-stage amplifier of Figure 9.1. End state: Figure 12.13 or Figure 12.14 on the patch, a live analog model of the regulator beside the hardware, and two compensation recipes from Chapter 13.

Rules

  1. Do not start a new box. Add a module or swap a socket.
  2. Each week ends with a measurement on the assembled machine, not on a scrap circuit.
  3. ICs first. Discrete amp later. It must drop into a socket the computer already uses.
  4. Use only the compensation capacitor declared for that week and configuration; socketed alternatives are mutually exclusive, never silently paralleled.
  5. Week 0: dual ±15 V, LM301A as the primary stock amplifier, breadboard or vectorboard that will still exist in week 14, scope, and Figure 11.7-style local decoupling. A 741-class implementation remains a documented alternative.
